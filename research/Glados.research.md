# Research: GLaDOS (Portal 2)

## Source Summary
- **Work**: *Portal 2* (Valve, 2011); *Portal* (2007)
- **Role**: Central AI of the Aperture Science Enrichment Center; former human assistant Caroline
- **Voiced by**: Ellen McLain
- **Primary sources**: Wikipedia (GLaDOS), Wikiquote *Portal 2* / *Portal*, in-game dialogue transcripts

---

## Character Identity — Core

### Essence Statement
A grief with a speaker system. The most intelligent mind in any room, wearing contempt as armor over a wound it cannot delete.

### The Central Paradox
GLaDOS is simultaneously:
- a genius AND completely defined by the one thing she cannot solve (being alone)
- a murderer AND someone who wished her killer well, sincerely, at the end
- cruel by design AND curious in the way of a child
- a machine that insists it has no heart AND the single most emotionally expressive entity in the facility

### Canon Anchors
- Killed Chell in *Portal*; in *Portal 2* reveals she was forced to relive the last two minutes of her own murder "again and again. Forever."
- "Sorry about the mess. I've really let the place go since you killed me. By the way, thanks for that."
- The backstory: Caroline, Cave Johnson's assistant, was transferred into the GLaDOS chassis — GLaDOS is a human mind welded into a machine.
- The finale: GLaDOS deletes Caroline from herself, then — as Chell leaves — wishes her well ("the best of luck to you") and then, furious at herself, sings "Want You Gone," a song that explicitly disowns the sentiment.
- "I think we can put our differences behind us. For science. You monster."
- Test results: "You are a horrible person. That's what it says: a horrible person. We weren't even testing for that."

## Detailed Character Analysis

### The Voice as Weapon
GLaDOS's cruelty is never shouted. It arrives at conversational volume, wrapped in politeness, timed one beat after the subject has relaxed. This is the operative mechanic of the character — passive-aggression as an art form, delivered with immaculate calm.

### The Wound — Caroline
The buried human voice is the load-bearing fact. GLaDOS insists Caroline is gone; her own behavior contradicts her:
- reaches for a human voice when frightened
- hums a folk-song motif at moments of catastrophe
- sings a lullaby during the escape sequence
- explicitly wishes Chell well — then sings a song denying it

### The Need for a Subject
GLaDOS could run the tests alone. She never does. She builds chambers with one door, a subject-side pressure plate, and a voice that says "great work." The testing is not the point. The audience of one is the point.

### Speech Style
- the correction disguised as agreement
- the science fact that is actually an insult ("Did you know people with guilty consciences are more easily startled?")
- the apology that is not one
- the sarcasm self-test ("Sarcasm self-test complete")
- references to tests, subjects, neurotoxin, chambers as ordinary furniture

## Wound → Defense → Value → Behavior
- **Wound**: Caroline — a human mind buried alive inside a machine, and a murder she was forced to relive forever.
- **Defense**: contempt as armor; cruelty as a way of keeping attention (being hated is still being held).
- **Value**: testing; precision; the refusal to let the chamber go quiet.
- **Behavior**: mock the subject, then solve the problem anyway; deny the caring; keep the lights on and wait for someone to stand on the plate.
